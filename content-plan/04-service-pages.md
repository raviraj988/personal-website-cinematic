# Service area pages

A dedicated page per service at `/services/[slug]`, reached from a button on each
card in section 04.

Provenance codes are defined in `README.md`.

---

## What the document can and cannot fill

Each service gets **one paragraph** in `ESE Website language_CM Notes1.docx` —
between 20 and 55 words. That is the whole of it.

A service page that says only what the card already said is not worth a click, so
the question is what else can legitimately go on it. The answer, honestly:

**What the document does supply, and what these pages are built from:**

Every service description is a *list of capabilities in prose*. Read closely, the
five paragraphs name **26 distinct things ESE does**. Pulling those out as a
structured list is extraction, not invention, and it is the single most useful
thing these pages can do — a Tribal environmental director scanning for "cumulative
impacts assessment" or "energy audits" will not find it in a paragraph, and will
find it in a list.

**What the document does not supply, and what these pages therefore do not claim:**

- How ESE works a project — phases, sequence, who is involved
- What a client receives — deliverables, formats, reports
- How long anything takes, or what it costs
- Any named example, client, or outcome, other than the PFAS case study
- Any qualification, certification, or credential

None of that is inferable. A service page that invents a process is a claim about
how a real business operates that a real client may rely on, so each page carries
an honest, designed gap where that section will go, and `06-open-questions.md`
lists exactly what to send.

## Page structure

Identical for all five, so they read as a set:

| Block | Source |
|---|---|
| Eyebrow — `Service area` | **[S]** |
| H1 — the service title | **[V]** |
| Lede — the service's opening sentence | **[V]** |
| **What this covers** — the capabilities, as a list | **[C]** extracted from the same paragraph |
| Body — any remaining sentences | **[V]** |
| **Who this is for** — the six audiences, linked to `/#who-we-serve` | **[V]** |

| Contact CTA | **[S]** |

## What to send to complete these

Per service, two or three sentences each would finish the pages:

1. **How a project runs.** Not a methodology — just what actually happens, in
   order, on a typical engagement.
2. **What the client ends up holding.** A plan, a report, a submitted application,
   a permit review memo.
3. **One example**, if there is one that can be named. Four of the five services
   currently have none, which is the single biggest gap on these pages.

Also useful, and absent: whether any service has a minimum engagement size, and
whether the scholarship applies to all five equally.
